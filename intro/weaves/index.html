Completion Is a Weave

The theorem the thread was rebuilding, and where it lives

Status: draft, unverified, not canon. Sixth in the thread: The Reversible One → Slot-Closure → One Curve (superseded) → The Anchor Was a Shadow → The Scalar That Doubles. This note is different in kind from the others. They built structure; this one identifies the structure they were building as something already known, and reads off what that buys and costs. It also carries three retractions of claims made earlier in the same thread — two of them mine, one of them a framing I proposed and then had to withdraw within the hour.

The thread kept converging on fibrations from directions that were not aiming at them: the sheets, π₁, the two decompositions, the double cover, Hopf. That was not a pattern-match. Making a many-to-one operation reversible is constructing the space it projects from — so a method built on refusing collapse will produce covering spaces whether or not it means to. The construction has a name, and stating it converts a research programme into a lookup.

The theorem

Define a weave as a series of interconnected transformations — morphisms mapping sets. Categorically: a functor into Set.

Then the classical result is Grothendieck's Galois theory, or in its topological form the covering-space correspondence: for a connected, suitably nice X, the covers of X are equivalent to the sets carrying a π₁(X)-action. And a π₁(X)-set is a functor from π₁(X), read as a one-object category, into Set. The definition of a weave and the target of the theorem are the same object.

Which upgrades the statement. Not "completion can be modelled as weaves" but
completion ≃ the category of weaves
an equivalence, not a representation. That distinction is the whole point here, because an equivalence loses nothing. "Information-conservative modelling" is precisely "equivalence of categories." The framework's central demand, which has been stated informally since Chapter 2 and flagged there as "not yet a fully formal criterion," has a one-line formal rendering.

It unifies three things that look unrelated: Galois field extensions, topological covering spaces, and étale covers of schemes. Which is worth noting, because the corpus has been reaching for all three vocabularies at once — "Galois twist" in papers/3, deck transformations in The Reversible One, monodromy throughout — and they are one theorem, not three analogies.

Reversibility picks the index category

A functor into Set is almost no constraint; nearly everything is one. The content lives in two places: which index category, and whether the morphisms must be isomorphisms. The framework answers the second.

If every operation must be reversible, the transformations in the weave are bijections. So the functor lands in the groupoid core of Set, not Set. A functor from a group into bijections of a set is a group action — a π₁-set — which is a covering. Reversibility selects the covering case by itself.

And consistency degrades in stages rather than failing all at once. Each stage gets a different object:

How much of the weave is bijectionsWhat you get
all of ita covering — fully reversible; index is a group
all but isolated pointsa ramified cover — reversible off the branch points
only locally coherenta weave of charts — reversible in patches; index is a site

That trichotomy is already in the corpus. papers/4 classifies charts three ways — the chart you can write, the chart you can solve, and "obstructed — irreducibly local — global monodromy: residue, associator, Écalle–Voronin." Écalle–Voronin moduli are exactly the third row: no global chart, and what survives is the gluing data between local ones. So that paper's three classes are not three observed cases. They are this theorem's trichotomy. Which also gives papers/0's "monodromy is one object wearing three faces" a cause rather than a coincidence: three faces because three grades of available structure.

The puncture is the price

The correspondence needs the map to be a local homeomorphism, and branch points break that: at a branch point the sheets collide instead of stacking.

In this framework the branch points are 0 and ω. Which explains three things that were noticed separately and never connected:

  • the carrier is ℂ* and not ℂ — the puncture is the removed branch point;
  • slot-closure named it "the puncture" on grounds having nothing to do with covering spaces;
  • the whole reversible structure exists because the two poles were excluded from the space they generate.

So the puncture is not a technicality to be tidied away later. Excluding 0 and ω is the price of a fully reversible weave, and the framework's demand and the puncture are the same decision seen twice.

The worked instance is the framework's own

log is many-valued on ℂ*. Its Riemann surface is ℂ, via exp: ℂ → ℂ*, with deck group ℤ acting by translations. That is the geometry which makes log reversible, and it is the textbook first example of the theorem.

It is also, exactly, the ℤ-sheet structure the previous note derived from 1/(−1) ≠ (−1)/1 without knowing what it was rebuilding. The residue R = n², the infinite thread-count, and the deck generator are one object arrived at from the wrong end.

The two logarithms

The grade is log₀. 0ⁿ sits at grade n, so log₀(0ⁿ) = n: the grading function and the base-0 logarithm are one thing under two names, and the framework has been carrying a working logarithm since Chapter 6 without calling it one.

The consequence that matters for zero divisors:

classical: log(0) = −∞ intractable here: log₀(0) = 1 finite, and a unit

Classical algebra cannot assign anything to a null element because its logarithm runs off the bottom. log₀ lands on 1. That is the structural reason the split-quaternion construction proposed in the previous note is plausible at all: det is multiplicative, so log₀ ∘ det is a grade, and the null cone acquires a finite one instead of an infinity.

Read off the ladder in quarter-turns — 1, 0, −1, ω at u = 0, 1, 2, 3:

actionas a map
log₀1↦0, 0↦1, ω↦−1, −1↦ωu ↦ 1 − u — a reflection about u = ½
logₑ1↦0, 0↦−1, −1↦ω, ω↦1u ↦ u + 1 — a quarter-turn rotation

log₀ reflects about a different axis than reciprocal does — r reflects about u = 0 — so log₀ is not in the Klein four-group {id, n, r, nr}. It enlarges the symmetry, which is what one wants from a new tool. And composing the two reflections gives a rotation:

log₀ ∘ r : u ↦ 1 − (−u) = u + 1 one quarter turn (log₀ ∘ r)² : u ↦ u + 2 = n negation

A quarter-turn squaring to negation is i. So i = log₀ ∘ r: two reflections about axes an eighth-turn apart, which is how a rotation always factors. Chapter 7 reaches i = 0^(ω/2) from a normalization; this reaches the same element from two operations, which is a second and independent route.

ω^ω was never a value

That cell refused to settle between 1 and −1 through six law-sets. The reason is one line:

ω^ω = 0^(−ω) = 1 / 0^ω = 1 / (−1)

It is a ratio — and specifically the denominator-negative one, the only position a negation can legitimately occupy. Asking whether it equals 1 or −1 was asking a ratio for a number, which is the same category error as asking a schema for a constant. The question was malformed, not the answer; and ω^ω is the one cell in the table that is intrinsically an ordered pair, which is why it is the cell where collapsing had to make a choice.

On the accounting, both orderings project to −1: r: u ↦ −u sends 2 ↦ −2, distinct upstairs by one full turn, identical below. Neither projects to 1. So the 1 answer came from the checkerboard pattern in the four-by-four table, which is a downstairs object throughout — the same class of evidence that produced the false agreement in One Curve and the 0² = −1 artefact in the curve diagram. It resolves to −1, and logₑ agrees: that map is a clean rotation only if ω^ω = −1; at 1 it goes multivalued and logₑ(−1) has no answer among the four.

The admissibility line is receipt, not vocabulary

A retraction, and it is mine. I argued that because ℝ is the completion of ℚ by limits and Chapter 2 refuses limits, analytic expressions are outside the framework. That is wrong. The criterion is total and reversible, and it cuts across the algebraic/analytic distinction rather than along it.

cos is total but many-to-one, so irreversible as stated — and the framework's standard move on an irreversible operation is to refuse the collapse and keep the receipt, which is exactly what The Reversible One does with its (mod 2π). That note was not reasoning analytically and mislabelling the results; it was applying the method to an analytic function, correctly. arccos multivalued with the winding retained is the reversible form of cos.

The line is expression versus value. Analysis keeps the value and discards the derivation; symbolic algebra keeps the expression and does not need the value. They are duals, and the Traction is the symbolic side. So cos(2πs) is admissible — the expression is retained, nothing was destroyed. 0.7071067811865476 is not: it is a value that has forgotten the expression that produced it, and √2/2 cannot be recovered from it. Floating point is excluded by the founding axiom rather than by taste — rounding is a merge with no receipt — and that is why there is no going back from it. Bare ℝ falls the same way; ℝ with its approximating sequence does not.

The correspondence principle

A guard stronger than any currently in these notes. Every upstairs distinction must vanish downstairs into exactly the classical value. The framework may add structure above; it may not disagree below.

What makes this the best falsifiability criterion in the thread is its reach. The two-floor rule tests the framework against itself — links collide or they do not. This tests it against the whole of existing mathematics, and a single downstairs disagreement is fatal. It also does immediate work: it confirms ω^ω ≈ −1, since both orderings project there and classical arithmetic agrees; and it puts the burden on the checkerboard's 1, which would have been a downstairs disagreement.

Where to work, and why Chebyshev

With the admissibility line at receipts rather than vocabulary, ℚ is not a boundary but the cheapest place to stand — a better claim, since it explains why the corpus gravitates there without defending a ban it does not need.

And it explains the recurrence of one object. cos, exp, and arccos need their receipts carried explicitly; Tₖ has integer coefficients and needs nothing. Chebyshev is the Traction's cosine. That is why it keeps surfacing from unrelated directions — the trace coordinate, the recurrence, the deck-invariant chart, the doubling constant — it is the receipt-free avatar of the circular functions. Likewise the "circle" of The Exact Circle is the roots of unity, torsion, cyclotomic; which is what exact was saying.

One consequence worth recording, since it sharpens a fork the thread stalled on for hours. Elliptic-versus-hyperbolic read analytically is a sign condition on a real, |s| < 2. Read arithmetically it is:

is s² − 4 a square in ℚ ?

If yes, ℚ(δ) = ℚ and the form is split; if no, ℚ(δ) is a quadratic extension and it is not. That is decidable for any given s, and it is what papers/3's "two real forms differing by a Galois twist" says once it is read arithmetically rather than analytically: the twist is the quadratic character of s² − 4. A fork becomes an algorithm.

What this note withdraws

  1. The analytic exclusion. Mine, and wrong: analytic expressions are admissible when their receipts are kept. The flags I placed on The Reversible One's tower and on log₁ = (1/2π)·arccos come off.
  2. The ℚ-only framing. Weakened to a claim about cost, not admissibility.
  3. ω^ω as undetermined. The Anchor Was a Shadow lists it as a fiber to be indexed. It is a ratio, and it resolves.

Three retractions here, on top of three in the two notes before it — R = (nr)², the objection to the half-turn placement, and totality-selects-ℇ. The pattern is worth stating plainly: every one was a structural argument that felt forced and was not, and every one was caught by checking against the corpus rather than against intuition. The results that have survived attack are the ones with computations behind them: |det| = 2, ω² = sω − 1, and the u-coordinate maps above.

Open

  1. Define weave to one row. As stated it names a shape spanning all three rows of the trichotomy — covering, ramified cover, moduli of local charts — which are genuinely different objects. The statement becomes a theorem where weave names one, or names a category containing all three with the maps between them. If it is the third row specifically, the claim is stronger than the classical theorem: that the obstructed case is still modelable, not merely obstructed. That is the version worth chasing.
  2. Which index category, in the framework's own case? Reversibility forces isos, hence a group, hence a covering — but only away from the branch points. What indexes the weave at 0 and ω is unaddressed, and those are the two elements the whole construction is about.
  3. Does the correspondence principle hold everywhere? It is strong enough to be worth attacking. Sweep the corpus for downstairs claims that disagree with classical arithmetic; each is either an error or an upstairs value wearing an equals sign.
  4. Is log₀'s new reflection compatible with the eight pivots? It sits outside the Klein four-group, so it enlarges the symmetry — and the pivot count was computed for the four curves alone. Adding a fifth generator may change |det| and therefore the count.
  5. Do the two completions commute? This note completes an operation by building the space it projects from — a covering, with ℂ* as base. The previous note completes a different operation by making reciprocal an element, which forces the algebra up to ℇ. These are two different completions of two different failures, at two different levels, and nothing so far says they combine into one structure rather than pulling apart. ℇ is not a covering of ℂ*. Whether the covering completion and the doubling completion are compatible — and if so what the combined object is — is a gap between the two notes, not inside either, which is exactly the sort of gap that stays invisible.
  6. Re-read The Reversible One from the top. Two flags placed on it today were both wrong, which means it has not actually been re-read, only reacted to. Its diagnosis — that x⁰ = 1 is the liar — is purely algebraic and survives everything; the material after "The tower" has never been audited against the discipline the thread has since developed.
  1. Provenance. The framework's: the correspondence principle; receipt-versus-value as the admissibility line and analysis as the dual of symbolic algebra; the definition of a weave and the statement that consistent completions are modelable as weaves; "find a compatible geometry alongside it"; that ω^ω is a ratio rather than a value; and the expectation that the logarithms would be the tool. Reconstruction, and unaudited: the identification with Grothendieck's Galois theory; the u-coordinate computation of log₀ and logₑ and the factorization i = log₀ ∘ r; reversibility-forces-isomorphisms; the identification of 0 and ω as the branch points and the puncture as its price; the mapping of the trichotomy onto papers/4; Chebyshev as the receipt-free avatar; and the fork as a rational-square condition.
