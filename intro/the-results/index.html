Chapter 1 · The Shock

The Results

Five identities, stated bare. None of them is a typo.

This book deletes one axiom of ordinary arithmetic and changes nothing else. What follows is what falls out. Each result below is stated first and explained later — the chapter that cashes it is named on the spot, and every claim wears its honest badge: Proven for a theorem or a machine-checked result, Plausible for a load-bearing bet stated as one, Wild for speculation. Read the equations. If any of them bothers you, that is the correct reaction, and the book exists to resolve it.

Zero has a reciprocal — and it is not infinity

0 · ω = 1

This is a definition, not a theorem: ω := 1/0, pinned by that single line. The proof that no such ω can exist uses exactly the axiom we deleted, and nothing else. ω is not a size that blew up; it is zero's exact algebraic partner, bounded and finite — doubling the exponent brings it back around, which infinity never does. Cashed in The Invertible Zero.

Minus one is a power of zero

0ω = −1

A choice, not a discovery — the one value the rules leave free, and this is the assignment that closes the system in balance. It is the fingerprint of a chosen multiplication, and the book says so every time it uses it. Cashed in Powers of the Zero; the choice itself is examined in Where the Choices Show.

The imaginary unit is a zero seen edge-on

i = 0ω/2

Not an assumption — a consequence of the previous line. If 0ω = −1, then the half-power squares to −1, which is the imaginary unit's entire job description. Nobody has to invent i here; it was hiding inside zero. Cashed in The Imaginary Unit.

The circle, exactly, with no limits

0ω/3 = 1/2 + (√3 / 2) i

A third of a half-turn, computed exactly — by hand, from a three-line recurrence — with no series, no radians, no limit anywhere in the calculation. Roots of unity fall out the same way, one integer polynomial at a time. Cashed in The Exact Circle.

Differentiation is multiplication by zero

f′(x) = P( ω · [ f(x + 0) − f(x) ] )

A derivative with no limit in it. Because 0·5 is no longer erased — the deleted axiom is exactly the one that erased it — f(x+0) carries the slope as a genuine, recoverable coefficient, and ω lifts it back up. The P is a projection, and it is doing honest work that a later chapter makes precise. Cashed in The Structural Differential.

The price of admission

All of it costs exactly one thing. Ordinary arithmetic contains an axiom — absorption, the rule that 0·a = 0 for every a — which quietly destroys information: multiply by zero and the a is gone, unrecoverable, the only irreversible step in all of elementary algebra. This book deletes that axiom and keeps everything else. Every result above is what arithmetic looks like once nothing is ever thrown away. The rest of the book earns them in order — and the rules of the game, stated in full, wait at the end, where you can check that nothing you just read was a trick.
