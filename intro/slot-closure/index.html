The Slot-Closure Formulation

Eight schemas, joined not resolved — and the fork underneath them

Status: draft, unverified, not canon. This is the successor thread to The Reversible One. Where that note found x⁰ = 1^x by chasing a single contradiction, this one asks the systematic question behind it: if certain identities are refused because writing them destroys structure, which identities close the system without collapsing it? Two structural pieces below are provisional and flagged as such. Read it as a lab notebook.

The governing rule is the same one that runs the whole project: information conservation, not mere consistency. A system can be consistent by being small. The demand here is stronger — operations must not destroy distinguishability, and the algebra must stay total. The methodological consequence took a while to absorb: some classically valid identities are refused, not because they are false, but because writing them collapses structure that should be kept. The model to keep in mind is split-complex arithmetic, where j² = 1, j ≠ 1 refuses to reduce to j = 1 — and that refusal is exactly what makes the object larger than ℝ.

Objects: slots, not terms

The carriers are not expressions but schemas — expressions with one free parameter. A term like (−1)⁰ is not in a class; it is the value of a schema at a point. Two schemas passing through the same point no more merge than two crossing lines become one line. (An earlier reading treated these as an equivalence relation on terms, let transitivity run through shared points, and collapsed eight objects to four. That was the wrong object.)

Closure means accounting for all eight schemas over the alphabet {0, ω, 1, −1} — each special value c contributing both c^x and x^c:

0^x ω^x 1^x (−1)^x x^0 x^ω x^1 x^(−1)

Each schema must belong to an equivalence class, additional identities may be introduced freely, and — the one hard constraint — no identity may carry a side condition of the form x ≠ y.

Join, don't resolve. x¹ = x and x⁰ = 1 are barred — not oversights. 4 + 3 collapses to 7; 4 + x has nowhere to go. x¹ = x is not a relation between two slots, it is an evaluation: it discharges a slot to something outside the exponential language, and a discharged slot can no longer serve as a link. Every slot must be joined to another schema, never resolved to arithmetic. Each schema must also reference its parameter exactly once — the expression must be a function of x, not a collapse of it.

Pivots as the only inference rule

With evaluation barred, the sole way to derive anything is a pivot: where two schemas cross — share a point — they are constrained to agree there. This is not a collision to be eliminated; it is data, and it propagates. The theorem set is exactly the transitive closure over shared points, so the size of the derived table is fixed entirely by the crossing pattern of the eight schemas. Few crossings means a sparse table with most cells undetermined — and undetermined is a finding, not a failure.

Not every new equivalence costs the same. The grading:

There is also a budget. On a finite carrier a schema pinned at enough points is determined, so two schemas may safely cross once, perhaps twice; three crossings and they are probably the same curve. Lines intersecting at points, not overlapping on intervals.

The carrier: −ω ≠ 0

The pivotal move of the thread: build the system in which negated-zero and ω are distinct. This dissolves a standing valuation conflict rather than patching it. With val(0) = +1 one has val(−0) = +1 but val(0^(−1)) = −1; these contradict only under the extra identification ω = −0 = 0^(−1), which forces +1 = −1. Drop that identification and both valuations are simply the valuations of two different elements. The inconsistency was never a bug; it was an unread proof that −0 and 0^(−1) are distinct. Whether the negated constants are separate anchors or negation is a schema-operation is still open.

The candidate closure

Four links cover all eight slots, once each, none discharged:

L1: 1^x = x^0 L2: 0^x = ω^(−x) L3: (−x)^ω = x^(−1) L4: x^1 = (−1)^(−x)

L1 is the x⁰ = 1^x insight from the previous note, now read as a link between two slots rather than a lone identity. L1–L3 are settled in shape; L4 is the weak point. Its pairing is forced — after L1–L3 only x¹ and (−1)^x remain, with nowhere to go but each other — but the sign (straight vs negated argument) was chosen on aesthetic grounds, not derived. It is very likely the branch selection; see the fork below.

The four links close into a cycle, each pair meeting exactly once, within budget:

PivotYields
L1 × L2 at 0⁰1⁰ = ω^(−0)
L2 × L3 at ω^ω0^(−ω) = (−ω)^(−1)
L3 × L4 at (−1)^ω(−ω)¹ = 1^(−1)
L4 × L1 at 1¹1⁰ = (−1)^(−1)

The first genuine output — a consequence, not an input — comes from combining the first and last pivots:

ω^(−0) = (−1)^(−1)

This links the signed sector to base −1. It is lines meeting at points, not curves overlapping, so it is information rather than collapse. The configuration satisfies the stated closure conditions; whether it is the solution or merely a solution is open, and hangs on L4.

The dual, and its fixed curve

There appear to be exactly two closing solutions, related by an involution ρ — "the dual lives in phase space." A bare relabeling would do no work, so a substantive dual needs a fixed structure to reflect about. The candidate is a fixed curve:

1^(−1) = 1^1

Base-1 is even in its exponent, and that evenness is ρ-invariant. Two things make it the right kind of fixed point. It is a fixed curve, not a fixed carrier element — enough to make the branches genuine reflections. And it projects to the classical numbers cleanly: since 1ⁿ = 1 for all n, evenness in the exponent is vacuously true downstairs. That suggests the axis of reflection is the classical part, with the deformation carried by the moving curves — which would explain why base-1 broke every earlier table: it was being asked to carry content that belongs on the axis, where content cannot live.

Conjecture. ρ has exactly one fixed curve — base-1, witnessed by 1^(−1) = 1^1 — so the two closing chains are mirror branches, not relabelings. Test: run the mirror chain (flip L4's sign), close it, and see which curves return to themselves. More than one fixed → the axis is too big and the dual is nearly a relabeling. Only base-1 → the conjecture holds.

The fork underneath — the thing worth deciding first

A separate rebuild routes the whole structure through a valuation: take val to be integer-valued, trivial on the rationals, with val(0) = +1. Grant that, and two good things follow. Chebyshev stops being posited. Let σ be the involution 0 ↔ ω; its fixed subring is generated by s = 0 + ω, and the traces tₙ = 0ⁿ + ωⁿ obey t₍ₙ₊₁₎ = s·tₙ − t₍ₙ₋₁₎ — the Chebyshev recurrence, tₙ = 2Tₙ(s/2). Chebyshev is just the trace coordinate of the norm-one subgroup N(x) = x·σ(x), where N(0) = 0·ω = 1. And the branch involution gets a name: ρ is s ↦ −s, trivial exactly at s = 0, which is exactly where −ω = 0. So dropping −ω = 0 and freeing s are the same move.

But grant the valuation and the geometry flips. val(0) = +1 is the valuation at the place s = ∞: it forces s to be ω-sized, δ² = s² − 4 a genuine square, and the norm form becomes the unit hyperbola. Elliptic becomes hyperbolic, i = 0^(ω/2) loses its referent, and the quadratic tower gives coquaternions, not quaternions. The whole complex corner of Chapters 7–9 is at stake.

Here is the point the two branches of this note turn on, and the reason to settle it before polishing L4. All of those costs come from val(0) = +1 — that 0 is an infinitesimal — and that is separable from −ω ≠ 0. The move −ω ≠ 0 only says s ≠ 0. For s in the range (−2, 2) the system stays elliptic: the norm form is still a circle, i is still present, and choosing an irrational angle makes ω infinite-order without any valuation at all. So one can keep −ω ≠ 0, keep the circle, and keep i, all at once — a regime the valuation rebuild skips over.

And yet val(0) = +1 is not an arbitrary import: it is precisely the grade from Powers of the Zero, where 0ⁿ sits at grade +n. The canon already assigns it. Which means the real tension is not in this extension but in the existing theory: canon wants both a genuinely infinitesimal, graded 0 (which pulls hyperbolic) and 0^ω = −1 with i = 0^(ω/2) (which pulls elliptic). The likely reconciliation is the puncture from the previous note: the integer powers 0ⁿ shrink — graded, infinitesimal, all the trace algebra can see — while 0^ω is the wrap, living in a sector the trace ring cannot represent. If that holds, the valuation rebuild is not refuting the circle; it is showing the circle never lived in the integer-power ring to begin with.

What is owed

  1. The fork itself. Decide, explicitly, whether 0 is an infinitesimal (graded, hyperbolic, coquaternions) or a unit on the circle (elliptic, i survives) — or whether the two sectors genuinely separate along integer-power vs wrap. Everything else is downstream.
  2. Carrier status of the negated constants. Anchors, or a schema operation? The whole signed sector rests on this.
  3. L4's sign. Probably the branch selection — and if ρ really is s ↦ −s, then "which sign in L4" and "which of the two dual solutions" are the same question with a coordinate attached. Testable via the mirror chain.
  4. The 0^ω = −1 replacement criterion. The four-element bijection argument that fixed it dies once the carrier grows past four; no replacement has been proposed.
  5. Base-1 over-determination. Evenness plus L1 pins 1¹ = 1^(−1) = 1⁰ — three points on a small carrier, halfway to fixing the curve. Needs checking against the pivot budget.
