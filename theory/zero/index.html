Something from Nothing

The first algebra trick you learned in grade school was essentially creating something from nothing. You could have an equation, add the same number to both sides, and the equality still holds. This concept has a deep parallel in physics. Solar panels for example absorb photons, which excite electrons, forming "electron-hole pairs" - the basis of photovoltaics. The common thread is information conservation.

"Energy can't be created or destroyed" is the most reliable physical law in the universe, yet our mathematics doesn't respect this law. Multiply both sides of an equation by zero, then you'll see: destruction in mathematics is as simple as the number zero. This has real consequences.
